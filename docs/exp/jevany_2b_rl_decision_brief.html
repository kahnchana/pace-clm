Research decision brief · revised 05 November 2026 · four H200 GPUs

Teach the 2B controller
when a response is worth making.

Three actions. Short rollouts. One frozen answer generator. Continue the same decoder LoRA and pointer head trained by SFT. Test delayed credit on four H200 GPUs.

Agreed scope: every trained controller is Qwen3.5-2B. Answers come from the frozen promoted PACE MoE. This is a proposed implementation and experiment campaign; no RL jobs or API calls were launched while preparing this brief.

The recommended path

Model

Continue the trained 2B J1-S

Use the completed 10% run, not a fresh base model or a silently substituted full-data checkpoint. Train the existing decoder LoRA and q/k pointer head in every RL run, matching SFT. Freeze original base weights and vision. Verified: 62,521,344 LoRA parameters across all 24 decoder layers, plus 1,049,088 pointer parameters.

Learning

Categorical policy gradient

Sample Silence / Hold / Response directly from the three pointer logits. One on-policy update per rollout batch. No vocabulary projection, language-token RL, critic, or initial verl/SGLang integration.

Evaluation

Protect useful coverage

Optimize correct, early, distinct fires with an explicit missed-event penalty. Monitor closed-loop recall and wasted fires together. Keep GPT-4.1 judged PAUC-F as the final metric.

What we borrow: Self-Focus supplies shared-prefix groups and future-return credit; MMDuet2 motivates short multi-turn rollouts with correctness, timing and redundancy feedback. We add no sensing, retrieval or resolution actions. This is an adaptation to JevAny, not a reproduction of either paper.

Paper motivation and limits

Self-Focus: eight sampled rollouts share a reference prefix and the same incoming stream. Credit uses each trajectory's remaining return relative to other trajectories. Their sensing mechanism is outside this campaign. Their update does not normalize advantages by group standard deviation, and uses one update per batch. [S1]

MMDuet2: its paper holds out 1,500 WEB and 400 ego videos for RL, uses 20–60-second training segments, gold dialogue before the segment, four rollouts, and correctness/timing plus redundancy penalties. It reports eight H800 GPUs and about 20 hours for RL. Its token policy, frame cadence and PAUC-based reward differ from ours; the runtime is not a budget estimate for JevAny. The official release includes modified verl/SGLang code, useful as reference rather than a drop-in pointer trainer. [S2]

One rollout group, end to end

1 · Shared prefixSample one training span. Replay causal history up to its start.
2 · Four trajectoriesSame frames and question; independently sampled categorical actions.
3 · Actual answersOn Response, call frozen MoE. Feed its answer into later history.
4 · One updateScore trajectories, compute credit, aggregate all gradients, then update once.
  • Policy: π(a|h) = softmax(pointer_logits), temperature 1.0. Store action ID, all three probabilities, policy version, exact prompt/frame IDs, prior emitted text and memory. Evaluation remains greedy argmax with response margin 0.
  • Runtime: preserve J1 history, 1 FPS, 32-frame causal window, 384px / 144 visual tokens per frame, direct prefix and last-response v2 memory. Hold maps to the existing Standby tag. Do not use response-blind J2 inputs.
  • Data: build a versioned pool of 4,096 contiguous training spans from the same 10% video cohort. RL uses spans, not a second 10% sample or an epoch over the 263,760 SFT rows. Reserve validation videos outside RL training, audit video paths as well as IDs, and record annotation hashes.
  • Span proposal: start with 32 seconds; 50% single-event, 25% multiple/repeated-event, 25% no-event. Include full four-second reward windows and sufficient post-response tail; reject boundary-censored spans. Short clips may be shorter and must be masked. Expand to 64 seconds only in a later matched campaign.
  • Prefix: use the same causal reference prefix for all four samples; no gold history after the branch. Full-video development rollouts test the remaining reference-prefix mismatch. A policy-generated prefix burn-in is a later ablation.
  • Frozen environment: pin the promoted MoE adapter, greedy answer decoding and 96-token cap. Keep controller options out of generator prompts. Two runtimes and two feature stores are required: 2B and MoE embeddings cannot be interchanged.
Hold and Silence need attention. Neither action emits an answer, so outcome reward may weakly distinguish them. Their tags also alter later model inputs. Keep a three-way SFT-policy KL anchor, monitor both classes on annotated validation states, and test additional supervised replay rather than assigning a positive reward to waiting.

A reward we can audit and assign over time

Recommended first reward: additive event outcomes, not global PAUC-F. Full PAUC-F remains evaluation-only. A sum of settled events makes future-return credit well-defined; globally normalized F1 and retrospective Hungarian rematching do not naturally yield immutable per-step rewards.

K = number of fully included annotated event windows
D = max(1, K)

R = [ Σ matched q_k × w_k
      − 0.50 × missed events
      − 0.25 × other unmatched fires
      − 0.35 × duplicate fires ] / D

q_k ∈ [0,1]: frozen semantic correctness score; accept match if q_k ≥ 0.5
w_k = max(0, 1 − delay_seconds / 4)
valid training window: [annotated onset, onset + 4 seconds)

The coefficients are proposed defaults, not tuned results or paper settings. Freeze them after toy-case checks and before the comparative runs. Use the existing MarkII four-second training-window convention; do not change official WEB windows.

  • Match fires chronologically to the earliest eligible, unmatched event window; each event can earn useful credit once. Overlapping-event tie rules must be deterministic. Distinct recurring events remain distinct even when answer wording repeats.
  • A fire is either matched, duplicate, or other unmatched; never charge both duplicate and unmatched penalties. A wrong early answer can be penalized and a later correct answer can still match. Prefix responses covering an event must be tracked so the same event is not credited again.
  • For delayed credit, settle each event's matched score or miss at its fixed window close; settle fire penalties at the fire. Settlement times are identical across trajectories. End the span after every scored window closes, making total R equal to the sum of its ledger entries.
  • On no-event spans, no fires earns 0; every fire incurs a penalty. Do not give a positive survival bonus for silence. Event spans make permanent silence negative through missed-event penalties. Track event strata separately so the sampled mixture cannot conceal lost recall.
  • Correctness must use the actual generated answer. Timing-only q=1 and lexical similarity are diagnostic backends, not the production reward. Reuse the local semantic-judge interface, calibrate on training-video cases against the official rubric, batch requests and cache by full request/model/rubric identity. A text judge cannot independently verify all visual claims; audit disagreements with frames.
Compare trajectory credit with delayed credit 
Run 1: A_i,t = R_i − mean(R_j for j ≠ i)
Run 2: A_i,t = R_i,remaining(t) − mean(R_j,remaining(t) for j ≠ i)

L = − mean_over_groups [ (1/G) Σ_i Σ_t A_i,t log π(a_i,t | h_i,t) / 32 ]
    + 0.02 × mean KL(π || π_SFT) − 0.001 × mean entropy(π)

Run 3 adds: 0.10 × action CE on a fixed training-only SFT replay batch

Include the current step's ledger contribution in remaining return. Treat returns and baselines as detached. Use unnormalized leave-one-out advantages in both runs, so the sole change is the credit horizon. A one-update on-policy estimator needs neither stored LM token ratios nor PPO clipping. This is group-relative REINFORCE/RLOO-style training; avoid presenting it as an unchanged stock GRPO implementation.

Use all action steps. An all-zero reward-credit group still counts in the denominator; any KL/entropy/CE terms remain defined. Equal terminal returns do not imply equal future returns. Keep sampling on-policy: no forced fires, action quotas, hidden top-k truncation or per-state exploration changes without recording the actual behavior distribution.

Expected limitation: all actions between event settlements may receive nearly the same outcome credit. Delayed credit removes already-settled events, but does not identify a unique causal action. With one event per span it may provide little benefit; the multi-event stratum is essential to this comparison.

Hold/Silence regularization and reference cost

Keep a frozen copy of the SFT adapter and pointer head as the reference. A worker may share the immutable base weights between current and reference adapters. Compute reference probabilities without gradients on the exact saved observations, and store those three probabilities; this needs a separate decoder forward. Then select the current adapter and replay each observation with gradients through the decoder LoRA and pointer head. Avoid adapter switching between a differentiable forward and its backward. Detached decoder features cannot replace this backward path.

Reference outputs may be cached by exact observation and frozen model identity; current decoder hidden features become stale after LoRA updates. Additional replay in run 3 uses existing J1-S labels on original gold histories, not copied labels on divergent on-policy states. Do not replace SFT-reference KL with old-policy KL while claiming the objective is unchanged.

Optimizer proposal: AdamW, pointer LR 1e-5, LoRA LR 1e-6, weight decay 0.01, global gradient norm 1.0; entropy 1e-3 and KL 0.02. The trainable parameter set matches SFT; RL learning rates need not equal SFT rates (1e-4 pointer / 2e-6 LoRA). Disable dropout consistently for rollout and likelihood replay while keeping gradients enabled for trainable weights. Accumulate microbatches and perform exactly one optimizer step after the complete rollout batch.

Measured memory anchor: x3 SFT probes report full-32-frame allocated peaks of 13.03 GiB at microbatch 4 and 38.94 GiB at microbatch 16. These exclude a co-located judge and do not guarantee RL peak memory. Start small, retain headroom, and profile the exact reference/actor path. No ZeRO/FSDP sharding is required merely to fit 2B at these batch sizes.

The first 3–5 experiments

All trained controllers are 2B. Every comparative run updates pointer + decoder LoRA and starts afresh from the same frozen SFT checkpoint and uses the same reward, span draw schedule, generator, training budget and greedy evaluation. Do not continue run 1 into run 2. The existing SFT checkpoint is the no-update control and does not consume a new training run.

Pass these gates before a full campaign

  1. Observation and probability parity. Identical action scripts reproduce existing 2B evaluator inputs, frame windows, memory and MoE answers. On saved traces, reconstruct categorical log-probabilities within declared BF16 tolerance (proposed max absolute log-prob error 0.02); use exact same weights and deterministic forward mode.
  2. Reward unit cases. Test all-silent, one early correct fire, one late correct fire, wrong fire, duplicate, recurring same-caption event, overlap, and no-event spans. Check additive ledger sum equals terminal reward and negative credit does not cross a settled boundary incorrectly.
  3. Credit/gradient gate. Equal future-return groups have zero reward-gradient contribution; sampling seeds produce action diversity; the chosen-action log-prob receives the intended gradient sign. Verify nonzero gradients in pointer and decoder LoRA, and zero gradients in original base weights, vision, SFT reference and generator. Exact three-way KL is nonnegative within numerical tolerance.
  4. One-update + resume gate. Complete the full pipeline, save pointer and LoRA, optimizer, RNGs, sampler cursor, policy version and completed-batch IDs, then restore. Do not replay a completed optimizer update or omit unfinished judge results.
  5. Throughput gate. Profile at least three warm updates separately: decode, vision, decision forward, generator queue, judge queue, learner, sync and save. Report wall time, GPU-hours, decisions/s, fires/s, queue idle time, cache hit rates and peak allocated/reserved memory. Project the entire campaign before launching.
  6. Closed-loop coverage gate. On a fixed 64-video RL-development cohort, compare to the exact 2B baseline with the same generator. Proposed pause conditions: matched useful-event coverage below 90% of baseline, fires above 1.5× with no coverage gain, or Hold/Silence macro-F1 loss above 5 points on annotated states. Stop at two consecutive checks, or immediately for a near-zero-fire collapse. Freeze thresholds before E1.

Development/confirmation manifests must be video-disjoint from training. Prefer fresh MarkII validation videos; if cohorts were inspected before, label them reused development rather than untouched test data. Tune on development only. Select a recipe/checkpoint before full WEB; use official judged PAUC-F and raw useful/wasted counts, not the training surrogate, to rank candidates.

Benchmark order: evaluate the frozen candidate and exact 2B SFT control on all 500 WEB videos, with GPT-4.1, pauc-f-v1, ω=0.5 and margin 0. Use paired per-video bootstrap of the recomputed global metric. WEB has already informed earlier work, so state that limitation. A final EGO check is needed for any cross-domain claim; it need not block the first WEB report.
